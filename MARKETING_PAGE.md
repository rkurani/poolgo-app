## What we do (three blocks)

### Customer communication
Branded SMS and email for every pool business. Stores send through their own number and domain. We handle delivery, replies, opt-outs, and compliance — they handle their customers.

### Connected records
One persistent record per pool. Tests, equipment, visits, purchases — kept current by every partner the homeowner has connected. Stays with the homeowner across providers, stores, and stays.

### Industry integrations
Photometers, service tech apps, OEM equipment, retail POS. One integration, available to every partner. Your customers get a unified experience without you having to build one.

## Voice & tone rules

- **No exclamation points.** Stripe doesn't use them. Confidence reads as flatness, not enthusiasm.
- **No em-dashes.** Per global drafting rules. Use commas, periods, or rewrite.
- **No "we believe" / "our mission" pablum.** State what's true, not what's aspirational.
- **No buzzwords.** No "synergy," "ecosystem," "transformation," "revolutionize," "platform of platforms." If a sentence would survive being read aloud to a pool store owner without making them roll their eyes, it stays.
- **Vague is fine in year 1.** "We power customer communication for the pool industry" is enough. Specificity comes as partners ship.
- **No consumer pitch.** Year 1 the page never says "find a pool store" or "manage your pool." That's year 3.

---

## What's deliberately missing

- "Manage your pool" / "Track your water" → consumer pitch. Not until year 3.
- A pricing page → we're not selling self-serve.
- A "request a demo" form with 15 fields → the partners we want are referrals and warm intros, not inbound demo requests.
- Investor / press / blog → no story to tell yet, and the page should feel like infrastructure, not a startup.
- Detailed product pages → partners we sign close in conversation, not in a feature matrix. Save the depth for the docs site (year 2+).

## Visual direction (notes, not a comp)

- **Layout:** Stripe's narrow, centered hero feel. Lots of negative space. Single column on mobile, never aggressively-wide on desktop.
- **Color:** PoolGo cyan (`#1FA0BF` from DESIGN.md) reserved for one or two accent moments — the headline period, a CTA outline. Otherwise grayscale on the white surface that already drives PoolGo Pure.
- **Type:** Inter Tight, same scale as the app — `--text-display` for hero, `--text-headline-md` for blocks. Consistency with the rest of the platform is its own form of credibility.
- **Imagery:** None until you have something honest to show. No stock photos of pools. No abstract gradient hero blocks. Logos and type carry the whole page in year 1.
